# Data sources

All data in this folder was retrieved on **2026-10-21** and processed by `scripts/fetch-layers.sh` (mapshaper 0.7.93), except the two files that say otherwise: `ecoregions.geojson` and `industry-waste.geojson`. Every geometry is clipped to the demo box `-123.75,48.28,-123.20,48.70` (west, south, east, north), simplified for the web and rounded to 5 decimal places (about 1 m). These are orientation layers, not survey or legal boundaries.

These notes cover the data. The MIT licence in [LICENSE](../LICENSE) covers the code only, and every layer below keeps its own licence.

## Filters and processing per layer

Feature counts are after clipping to the demo box. The count before clipping is in brackets where it differs.

- **municipalities**: rows where `ADMIN_AREA_GROUP_NAME` is "Capital Regional District". Keeps the abbreviation as `name`. Simplify 8%, keep shapes. Legal boundaries include adjacent water, and the popup says so.
- **bc-parks**: all 17 provincial parks and ecological reserves in the box. Keeps `PROTECTED_LANDS_NAME`, `PROTECTED_LANDS_DESIGNATION` and `PARK_CLASS`. Names are the Province's published names, displayed as published (capitals and the dual name `ȽÁUˌWELṈEW̱ / JOHN DEAN PARK` unchanged). Simplify 15%, keep shapes.
- **greenspaces**: rows where `REGIONAL_DISTRICT` is "Capital" and `PARK_TYPE` is not "Private" (1,111 rows), then the 79 rows that carry the Capital Regional District statement (`lic` "C") are dropped, leaving 1,032 (75 of those 79 would have remained after clipping and sliver removal). Slivers under 150 sq m and features left empty by clipping are dropped, leaving 934, which includes the one row that carries the Islands Trust statement (`lic` "I"). The fetch script drops only "C" before clipping. The Islands Trust row (SISȻENEM) in the committed file was put back on 2026-10-21 from the earlier local build of this file, without a refetch. Because the simplifier ranks vertices across the whole layer, a full refetch can differ from the committed file by a few vertices, in that row and in other parks. Keeps `name`, `type`, `use`, `muni` and `lic`. Simplify 20%, keep shapes. The simplifier reports 65 line intersections it could not repair, where parks overlap in the source. That is harmless for translucent fills.
- **streams**: named segments only (`GNIS_NAME` not empty), 592 before clipping and 578 after. Keeps `name` and `order` (stream order). Simplify 40%, keep shapes. The Freshwater Atlas has 12 named Bowker Creek segments (about 2.1 km of a creek the 2012 Bowker Creek Blueprint measures at 9.4 km), because piped stretches are unnamed or unmapped. The fetch also keeps `EDGE_TYPE`, `BLUE_LINE_KEY` and `DOWNSTREAM_ROUTE_MEASURE` in `raw/` (not committed), for the flow lines below.
- **streams-flow**: built from the clipped, simplified streams by `scripts/make-flow.mjs`. Three steps. (1) Direction: in the Freshwater Atlas the first vertex of a segment is its downstream end, so vertex order runs upstream. Measured on 2026-10-21 from the vertex heights of the raw download (592 named segments): the last vertex is higher than the first on 401, lower on 4, within half a metre on 187. Every line is reversed once, so the last vertex is downstream, which is the way MapLibre marches the dashes. The script re-checks the assumption on each run, using the distance from the mouth, and stops if it fails (on 2026-10-21 with canals added: 432 of 432 joins consistent). (2) Joins: segments of one stream with the same stream order that share an end point become one continuous line, so the dash pattern does not restart at every join. 556 segments became 124 lines. (3) Open channels only: edge types 1000 (single line main flow), 1050 (through a wetland), 1200 (construction line main flow, through a lake), 1250 (construction line, double-line river, main flow) and, since 2026-10-21, 2000 "Single line, Canal" (23 segments, 11.6 km, eleven creeks). Left out, so they get no dashes: 1425 "Construction line, subsurface flow" (11 segments, 1.3 km, Bowker Creek and Swan Lake Creek) and 1450 "Construction line, connection" (11 segments, 3.0 km). Edge type names are from the [Freshwater Atlas User Guide](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/data/geographic/topography/fwa/fwa_user_guide.pdf), table 2, read again on 2026-10-21, which also says the stream network holds "streams, tributaries and major rivers, canals, and ditches". The guide does not define a canal further, so the page calls them canals, as the Province does, and does not say whether they were dug or straightened. The kept lines are 331 of the 335 km of named creeks in the box. Bowker Creek keeps about 1,420 m of its roughly 2,110 m (484 m natural channel and 939 m canal, 67%), and the other 684 m, about 32%, is mapped as subsurface flow and stays undashed. The 2026-10-21 rebuild re-downloaded the stream network from the WFS (HTTP 200, 3.97 MB) and reproduced `streams.geojson` byte for byte before the flow file was rebuilt, so only `streams-flow.geojson` changed. The page checks the direction at two mouths: the last vertex of the Colquitz River line must be within 300 m of Portage Inlet (-123.41134, 48.45947) and the last vertex of the Bowker Creek line within 300 m of the Oak Bay shore (-123.30658, 48.42922), and that Bowker Creek's flow lines are between 1,300 and 1,600 m long (canals in, pipes out).
- **lakes**: 1 ha or more, 80 before clipping and 79 after. Keeps `name` and `area_ha`. Simplify 30%, keep shapes.
- **wetlands**: 0.5 ha or more, 205 before clipping and 203 after. Keeps `name` and `area_ha`. Simplify 30%, keep shapes.
- **watersheds**: named watersheds under 8,000 ha whose centroid is inside `-123.65,48.34,-123.22,48.66`. Keeps `name` and `area_ha` (rounded). Simplify 12%, keep shapes. Watersheds nest, so the page shows the smallest one under a click. The Province's Bowker Creek polygon is 1,381 ha. Other sources give other figures, so the popup says other groups draw watersheds differently.
- **bec**: 14 clipped polygons dissolved to one per biogeoclimatic label: CDF mm, CWH dm 1, CWH dm 2, CWH mm 1, CWH mm 2. Simplify 4%, keep shapes. The polygons extend over open water, so the page redraws the basemap's water above them.

## Terrain shading (not stored)

No elevation data is stored in this folder. The Terrain shading layer draws a hillshade in the browser from [AWS Terrain Tiles](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/) in terrarium encoding, loaded from `https://s3.amazonaws.com/elevation-tiles-prod/terrarium/{z}/{x}/{y}.png`. Terrain shading and 3D terrain are on by default (since 2026-10-21), so tiles are requested on load. With both boxes unticked, none are. The tiles are a composite. The `x-amz-meta-x-imagery-sources` header on Victoria-area tiles checked on 2026-10-21 names GMTED2010 and ETOPO1 at low zoom, then nrcan_cdem (Canada), srtm, USGS ned13, and at the eastern edge of the box ned19 San Juan County lidar. The attribution lines the page shows, from the [tile attribution notes](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md) ("Attribution is required for many terrain tile data providers. Example language is provided below, but you are responsible for researching each project to follow their license terms."):

- Canada terrain data contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada.
- United States 3DEP (formerly NED) and global GMTED2010 and SRTM terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey.
- Global ETOPO1 terrain data U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration.

The "Mapzen" line applies only "when using Mapzen's hosted service", not this bucket. Two cautions. The tile notes say the S3 tiles "are meant for efficient networking with EC2 resources only" and are not cached by a CDN, so a busy public site should host its own pre-extracted tiles. And a visitor who ticks the box sends tile requests to Amazon S3, which the credits panel says.

## Ecoregions

`ecoregions.geojson` holds one polygon, **Puget lowland forests** (biome Temperate Conifer Forests, realm Nearctic, eco_id 364), clipped to the demo box.

- Source: RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017. The page https://ecoregions.appspot.com/ (read 2026-10-21) shows "Ecoregions 2017 ©" (and "© Resolve" in its welcome text) and, beside its shapefile download (https://storage.googleapis.com/teow2016/Ecoregions2017.zip, 149,248,653 bytes, last modified 2019-05-07), "Licensed under CC-BY 4.0". The report it links is "An Ecoregion-Based Approach to Protecting Half the Terrestrial Realm".
- How it was made: from `ecoregions-2017.pmtiles` in the Salish Sea Atlas (built with tippecanoe 2.79.0, zoom 0 to 8, `--simplification=8`). `pmtiles extract --bbox=-123.75,48.28,-123.20,48.70` gave nine tiles, the box falls in one z8 tile (8/40/88), whose four ecoregion polygons were decoded to GeoJSON with @mapbox/vector-tile (the Atlas's own copy) and clipped to the box with mapshaper 0.7.93 (`-clip bbox=-123.75,48.28,-123.20,48.70`, precision 0.00001). The other three polygons in that tile (British Columbia coastal conifer forests, Central Pacific Northwest coastal forests, North Cascades conifer forests) lie outside the box.
- Cross-check: the RESOLVE UTFGrid tile for 8/40/88 (https://storage.googleapis.com/teow2016/Ecoregions2017/8/40/88.json) gives "Puget lowland forests" at downtown Victoria, Sooke, Metchosin and north Saanich, and nothing over water at Haro Strait and the Strait of Juan de Fuca. The 150 MB official shapefile was not downloaded. A change was made to the data (generalised by the tile build, clipped, three attribute names shortened), and the credits say so.
- Caveat: the shape is coarse (about 30 vertices for the whole outline inside the box). The page redraws the basemap's water over it so the fill stays on land, as it does for the BEC zones.
