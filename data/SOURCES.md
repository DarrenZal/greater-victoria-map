# Data sources

All data in this folder was retrieved on **2026-10-21** and processed by `scripts/fetch-layers.sh` (mapshaper 0.7.93), except the nine files that say otherwise: `ecoregions.geojson`, `industry-waste.geojson` and the seven marine files (retrieved **2026-10-22**, see "Marine life and protection" below). Every geometry is clipped to the demo box `-123.75,48.28,-123.20,48.70` (west, south, east, north), simplified for the web and rounded to 5 decimal places (about 1 m), except the seven marine files, which are clipped to the area the map reaches (`-124.4,47.9,-122.7,49.1`) and rounded to 4 decimal places (about 8 by 11 m). These are orientation layers, not survey or legal boundaries.

## Filters and processing per layer

Feature counts are after clipping to the demo box. The count before clipping is in brackets where it differs.

- **municipalities**: rows where `ADMIN_AREA_GROUP_NAME` is "Capital Regional District". Keeps the abbreviation as `name`. Simplify 8%, keep shapes. Legal boundaries include adjacent water, and the popup says so.
- **bc-parks**: all 17 provincial parks and ecological reserves in the box. Keeps `PROTECTED_LANDS_NAME`, `PROTECTED_LANDS_DESIGNATION` and `PARK_CLASS`. Names are the Province's published names, displayed as published (capitals and the dual name `ȽÁUˌWELṈEW̱ / JOHN DEAN PARK` unchanged). Simplify 15%, keep shapes.
- **greenspaces**: rows where `REGIONAL_DISTRICT` is "Capital" and `PARK_TYPE` is not "Private" (1,111 rows), then the 79 rows that carry the Capital Regional District statement (`lic` "C") are dropped, leaving 1,032 (75 of those 79 would have remained after clipping and sliver removal). Slivers under 150 sq m and features left empty by clipping are dropped, leaving 934, which includes the one row that carries the Islands Trust statement (`lic` "I"). The fetch script drops only "C" before clipping. The Islands Trust row (SISȻENEM) in the committed file was put back on 2026-10-21 from the earlier local build of this file, without a refetch. Because the simplifier ranks vertices across the whole layer, a full refetch can differ from the committed file by a few vertices, in that row and in other parks. Keeps `name`, `type`, `use`, `muni` and `lic`. Simplify 20%, keep shapes. The simplifier reports 65 line intersections it could not repair, where parks overlap in the source. That is harmless for translucent fills.
- **streams**: named segments only (`GNIS_NAME` not empty), 592 before clipping and 578 after. Keeps `name` and `order` (stream order). Simplify 40%, keep shapes. The Freshwater Atlas has 12 named Bowker Creek segments (about 2.1 km of a creek the 2012 Bowker Creek Blueprint measures at 9.4 km), because piped stretches are unnamed or unmapped. The fetch also keeps `EDGE_TYPE`, `BLUE_LINE_KEY` and `DOWNSTREAM_ROUTE_MEASURE` in `raw/` (not committed), for the flow lines below.
- **streams-flow**: built from the clipped, simplified streams by `scripts/make-flow.mjs`. Three steps. (1) Direction: in the Freshwater Atlas the first vertex of a segment is its downstream end, so vertex order runs upstream. Measured on 2026-10-21 from the vertex heights of the raw download (592 named segments): the last vertex is higher than the first on 401, lower on 4, within half a metre on 187. Every line is reversed once, so the last vertex is downstream, which is the way MapLibre marches the dashes. The script re-checks the assumption on each run, using the distance from the mouth, and stops if it fails (on 2026-10-21 with canals added: 432 of 432 joins consistent). (2) Joins: segments of one stream with the same stream order that share an end point become one continuous line, so the dash pattern does not restart at every join. 556 segments became 124 lines. (3) Open channels only: edge types 1000 (single line main flow), 1050 (through a wetland), 1200 (construction line main flow, through a lake), 1250 (construction line, double-line river, main flow) and, since 2026-10-21, 2000 "Single line, Canal" (23 segments, 11.6 km, eleven creeks). Left out, so they get no dashes: 1425 "Construction line, subsurface flow" (11 segments, 1.3 km, Bowker Creek and Swan Lake Creek) and 1450 "Construction line, connection" (11 segments, 3.0 km). Edge type names are from the [Freshwater Atlas User Guide](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/data/geographic/topography/fwa/fwa_user_guide.pdf), table 2, read again on 2026-10-21, which also says the stream network holds "streams, tributaries and major rivers, canals, and ditches". The guide does not define a canal further, so the page calls them canals, as the Province does, and does not say whether they were dug or straightened. The kept lines are 331 of the 335 km of named creeks in the box. Bowker Creek keeps about 1,420 m of its roughly 2,110 m (484 m natural channel and 939 m canal, 67%), and the other 684 m, about 32%, is mapped as subsurface flow and stays undashed. The 2026-10-21 rebuild re-downloaded the stream network from the WFS (HTTP 200, 3.97 MB) and reproduced `streams.geojson` byte for byte before the flow file was rebuilt, so only `streams-flow.geojson` changed. The page checks the direction at two mouths: the last vertex of the Colquitz River line must be within 300 m of Portage Inlet (-123.41134, 48.45947) and the last vertex of the Bowker Creek line within 300 m of the Oak Bay shore (-123.30658, 48.42922), and that Bowker Creek's flow lines are between 1,300 and 1,600 m long (canals in, pipes out).
- **lakes**: 1 ha or more, 80 before clipping and 79 after. Keeps `name` and `area_ha`. Simplify 30%, keep shapes.
- **wetlands**: 0.5 ha or more, 205 before clipping and 203 after. Keeps `name` and `area_ha`. Simplify 30%, keep shapes.
- **watersheds**: named watersheds under 8,000 ha whose centroid is inside `-123.65,48.34,-123.22,48.66`. Keeps `name` and `area_ha` (rounded). Simplify 12%, keep shapes. Watersheds nest, so the page shows the smallest one under a click. The Province's Bowker Creek polygon is 1,381 ha. Other sources give other figures, so the popup says other groups draw watersheds differently.
- **bec**: 14 clipped polygons dissolved to one per biogeoclimatic label: CDF mm, CWH dm 1, CWH dm 2, CWH mm 1, CWH mm 2. Simplify 4%, keep shapes. The polygons extend over open water, so the page redraws the basemap's water above them.

## Terrain shading (not stored)

No elevation data is stored in this folder. The Terrain shading layer draws a hillshade in the browser from [AWS Terrain Tiles](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/) in terrarium encoding, loaded from `https://s3.amazonaws.com/elevation-tiles-prod/terrarium/{z}/{x}/{y}.png`. Terrain shading and 3D terrain are on by default (since 2026-10-21), so tiles are requested on load. With both boxes unticked, none are. The tiles are a composite. The `x-amz-meta-x-imagery-sources` header on Victoria-area tiles checked on 2026-10-21 names GMTED2010 and ETOPO1 at low zoom, then nrcan_cdem (Canada), srtm, USGS ned13, and at the eastern edge of the box ned19 San Juan County lidar. The attribution lines the page shows, from the [tile attribution notes](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md) ("Attribution is required for many terrain tile data providers. Example language is provided below, but you are responsible for researching each project to follow their license terms."):

- Canada terrain data contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada.
- United States 3DEP (formerly NED) and global GMTED2010 and SRTM terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey.
- Global ETOPO1 terrain data U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration.

The "Mapzen" line applies only "when using Mapzen's hosted service", not this bucket. Two cautions. The tile notes say the S3 tiles "are meant for efficient networking with EC2 resources only" and are not cached by a CDN, so a busy public site should host its own pre-extracted tiles. And a visitor who ticks the box sends tile requests to Amazon S3, which the credits panel says.

## Seafloor depth (not stored)

This section is about the ETOPO1 colours, which are the whole layer outside Canadian waters and the layer under the Canadian Hydrographic Service tiles inside them (see "Canadian Hydrographic Service" below). Nothing is stored. The Seafloor depth layer colours the sea in the browser with MapLibre's `color-relief` layer over a third elevation source (`seafloor`, with its tiles limited to zoom 8) on the same address as the other two, `https://s3.amazonaws.com/elevation-tiles-prod/terrarium/{z}/{x}/{y}.png`, so no new host. The depth is the ETOPO1 grid, which the tile notes (https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/data-sources.md, read 2026-10-22) describe as "ETOPO1 for ocean bathymetry, 1 arc-minute resolution globally" and say fills where GMTED and SRTM put 0 for water. A minute of arc is about 1.85 km north to south and 1.2 km east to west at 48.4 N. The credits and the on-map line say "ETOPO1, NOAA", "coarse" and "not for navigation".

The tile limit was chosen by measuring, on 2026-10-22, how much of the sea is still depth in the tiles at each zoom. For the tile over Victoria's waters (and for 10/160/354, over the east end of the Strait of Juan de Fuca): zoom 6 (10/22) 4% of pixels below -1 m, zoom 7 (20/44) 14%, zoom 8 (40/88) 47% with 4,087 distinct heights and a lowest point of -839 m (the tile reaches the open Pacific), zoom 9 (80/177) 31%, 3,707 heights, lowest -244 m, zoom 10 (161/354) 44% and 2,229 heights (160/354: 7%, 1,099 heights, lowest -173 m), zoom 11 (322/708) 52 pixels (under 0.1%) below -1 m and 905 heights (a lowest point of -4 m), zoom 12 (645/1417) one pixel. Looked at in the browser, the colours from the zoom 9 and zoom 10 tiles leave a large hole round Victoria's waters, where the Canadian and U.S. land data covers the sea and puts it at 0 m, so the zoom 8 tile is the last whole one. Overzoomed from it, the colours are smooth at every zoom, and from tile zoom 11 no sea is left to draw. Below 10 m nothing is coloured where only this grid reaches, because it puts the shore a kilometre or so out, and the colours fade in from 10 m to 25 m. The ramp (`DEPTH_STOPS` in [js/overlays-data.js](../js/overlays-data.js)): 25 m (150, 202, 222), 50 m (105, 170, 214), 100 m (66, 130, 192), 200 m (44, 92, 160), 300 m (32, 66, 128) and deeper the same, at 0.75 opacity, chosen by eye on the pale basemap sea so that the particles of Sea currents stay readable over it.

## Canadian Hydrographic Service (not stored)

The owner, Darren Zal, accepted the Canadian Hydrographic Service (CHS) licences on 2026-10-22 for two uses in this demo: the NONNA bathymetry tiles for Seafloor depth and the Victoria Harbour tide gauge (station 07120 of the Integrated Water Level System, IWLS) for Tides and tidal currents. Both are asked for live from the visitor's browser, and no copy of either is kept in this repository, in a build or on any server of ours. **This is a non-commercial demo.** The IWLS licence bars derivative products made for commercial purposes, for sale or profit, and this map is not made for any of them. Neither is for navigation, and the page says so wherever they are shown. Nothing else from CHS is used: no other tide or current station, no chart, no other CHS layer.

**IWLS licence, read 2026-10-22** at https://tides.gc.ca/en/licence-agreement ("Date modified: 2023-08-14"). Quoted: "The copyrights of CHS in the Data remain the property of CHS and shall not be sold, licensed, leased, assigned or given to a third party." "The Data shall not be used in the purpose of producing derivative products for commercial purposes, for sale or profit under any form whatsoever." "The Data shall not be used in the purpose of navigation;" "Where the User creates a derivative product from the Data (not for profit under any form whatsoever and not for navigation) the User shall include the following notice in a prominent location on such derivative product:" and then the notice, which the credits, the README, the panel and the gauge popup carry word for word with "Darren Zal" in the place the licence leaves for the user's name. "When the Data is further defined by metadata, the User must take this metadata into consideration." "The User shall destroy all the User's copies of the Data within 15 days of termination." (Nothing is kept, so there is nothing to destroy.) The licence takes effect when the user "otherwise uses the Data" and ends when CHS posts a public notice on its site, and "All restrictions on the use of the Data set out in this Licence Agreement survive termination." The page about CHS web services (https://tides.gc.ca/en/web-services-offered-canadian-hydrographic-service, read the same day) says they are "available free of charge under license". Whether a public non-commercial demo is a derivative product at all is not settled by the text: the licence allows one that is "not for profit under any form" with the notice, which is what is done. The licence page gives a contact for questions.

**NONNA licence, read 2026-10-22**: the "CHS NONNA Licence", a PDF linked from the Open Government Portal record "Canadian Hydrographic Service Non-Navigational (NONNA) Bathymetric Data" (https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/d3881c4c-650d-4070-bf9b-1e00aabf0a1d, record modified 2026-05-15, data updated 2026-04-01 by the record), at https://api-proxy.edh-cde.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/catalogue/records/d3881c4c-650d-4070-bf9b-1e00aabf0a1d/attachments/CHS_NONNA_LICENCE-LICENCE_NONNA_DU_SHC.pdf. The record names the Open Government Licence - Canada and says to see the CHS NONNA licence "for the full terms". Quoted: "The copyright of CHS in the Data, and any permissions granted to CHS, remain the property of CHS and shall not be sold, licensed, leased, assigned or given to a third party." "The Data shall not be used for navigation. The User acknowledges that the Data does not meet the requirements of the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 under the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 nor will any product created by the User from the Data." "Where the User creates a derivative product from the Data, the User shall include the following notice in a prominent location on such derivative product:" and then the notice, carried word for word as above with "Darren Zal" as the user. "The User shall destroy all copies of the Data in their possession within 15 days of termination of this Licence." It has no ban on commercial derivative products, unlike the IWLS licence. Because the copyright clause forbids giving the data to a third party, no tile or grid is committed or published from here. The tiles reach the visitor's browser from CHS's own server, are painted again there and are not sent anywhere. The server tells the browser to keep a tile for a day (`cache-control: max-age=86400`), which is the visitor's own cache and not a copy held by this project.

**NONNA tiles (Seafloor depth).** Service: the WMTS of the CHS NONNA GeoServer, https://nonna-geoserver.data.chs-shc.ca/geoserver/gwc/service/wmts (capabilities read 2026-10-22: layers `nonna:NONNA 100` and `nonna:NONNA 10`, one style `raster` for each, tiles as `image/png` or `image/jpeg`, `access-control-allow-origin: *`). The page asks for `https://nonna-geoserver.data.chs-shc.ca/geoserver/gwc/service/wmts/rest/nonna:NONNA%20100/raster/EPSG:900913/EPSG:900913:{z}/{y}/{x}?format=image/png` (and `NONNA%2010` from map zoom 11), the service's standard Web Mercator grid with 256 pixel tiles and the row counted from the top, which is what MapLibre asks for. The grid called `EPSG3857` in the same capabilities is not that grid (its tile sizes are set by map scale, so its tile numbers are other places), and a tile from it will look like data but is not the tile asked for. The NONNA coverage is the whole of Canada's waters: the tiles are empty (transparent) over land and over the U.S. side of the border, where the ETOPO1 colours below them show. Depths are metres below chart datum (the record says all NONNA data is vertically referenced to Chart Datum). The 100 m layer is used below map zoom 11 and the 10 m layer from there, because at zoom 11.6 the 10 m tiles show channels and banks where the 100 m cells are squares (looked at on 2026-10-22). Sizes measured on 2026-10-22 in a desktop window (1280 by 800): the 100 m tiles of the opening view, 24 tiles of about 16 KB each, and the 10 m tiles at zoom 11.6, 48 tiles of about 43 KB each.

*Colours.* The service draws depth with one fixed ramp (its legend graphic shows a rainbow from red at 0 m through yellow, green and blue to navy at 6,000 m) and offers no other style, so there is no way to ask for this map's blues. The browser therefore reads each pixel's colour back to a depth and paints it again in this map's own key, which is also what the ETOPO1 colours below it use, so one key in metres covers the layer. The legend graphic's own colours do not match what the service draws, so the ramp was fitted on the picture: on 2026-10-22, 424,060 places in six areas (100 m grid: the east end of the Strait of Juan de Fuca, Haro Strait, the Saanich Peninsula and Inlet, the Strait of Georgia by the border and the west end of the Strait of Juan de Fuca. 10 m grid: the east end of the Strait of Juan de Fuca) where the service's depth grid (WCS, `nonna__NONNA 100 Coverage` and `nonna__NONNA 10 Coverage`, 32-bit floats, elevation negative below datum) and its picture (WMS `GetMap` of the same box and size) were read together. The colour is a straight line in red, green and blue between points at 0 m (195, 0, 0), 10 m (194, 194, 0), 15 m (0, 194, 0), 30 m (9, 102, 195), 100 m (0, 56, 149) and 1,000 m (2, 0, 107), with a root mean square error of 0.5, 0.7 and 1.2 out of 255 in the three channels. Heights above datum run the other way, from (195, 0, 0) to brown (148, 83, 0) at 100 m, and a colour nearer that line than the depth ramp is land. Read back, a colour gives the depth to about 0.1 to 0.25 m at the median and 90th percentile down to 30 m, 0.6 to 0.8 m and about 2.3 m at 30 to 100 m, and about 8 m and 21 to 23 m between 100 and 400 m, where one step of colour (a whole number in the blue or green channel) is 16 to 21 m of depth, so the deep end of the layer is in bands of about that size. Of 6,559 places the depth grid put at or above datum, 261 (4%) were read as sea (mostly within a metre of datum), and of 413,352 places 1.5 m or deeper, 277 (0.07%) were read as no depth. Shallower than 1 m is left uncoloured, which also leaves the drying shore and every height above datum alone. Quoted: "Users must comply with the terms and conditions included in the licence selected by each Data Publisher, and the licensing information included with each data download.", "In order to make attribution of use for owners of the data possible, the identifier of ownership of data must be retained with every data record shared onward for reuse." and "it is forbidden to use APIs to create denial of service, disruption in the service, and in general to interfere with the normal operations of GBIF and the Data Publishers." and, on citing, "Users are strongly encouraged to cite data retrieved from the GBIF network according to the recommended citation, as shown on the dataset, occurrence or download page". For a CC BY 4.0 record the popup shows the rights holder the record names (for iNaturalist records, usually the observer), as that licence asks for credit.
