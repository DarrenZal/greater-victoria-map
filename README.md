# Greater Victoria open map (demo)

A small web map of Greater Victoria, built by Claude, an AI model, for Darren Zal. It is a demo of an open, self-hostable alternative to embedding Google Maps for local groups in Greater Victoria. It shows watersheds, creeks (with animated flow direction), parks, ecosystem zones, terrain shading and a 3D terrain view (both on when the page opens) from open data, plus an optional layer for events and places. Optional layers add seafloor depth, ecoregions, industry and waste sites, marine life and protection (protected marine areas, killer whale critical habitat, a killer whale important area, a whale and porpoise density model and coastal habitat from an old survey), and live data from outside services (precipitation radar, sea currents, wind, sea temperature, sea saltiness, tides and tidal currents, weather alerts, air quality, current conditions, river gauges, earthquakes and life observations). It is a demo. It is not an official map of any group.

Status: published at https://darrenzal.github.io/greater-victoria-map/ (code in https://github.com/DarrenZal/greater-victoria-map). Built on 2026-10-21. Data retrieved 2026-10-21, except the marine life and protection files (2026-10-22, except the killer whale important area, 2026-10-23).

Repository name `greater-victoria-map`. The code is under the MIT licence, see [LICENSE](LICENSE) (Darren Zal, 2026). The MIT licence covers the code only. Each data layer keeps its own licence, recorded per layer in [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md), and the MIT licence does not relicense any of it. The vendored MapLibre library keeps its own BSD-3-Clause licence in [vendor/MAPLIBRE-LICENSE.txt](vendor/MAPLIBRE-LICENSE.txt), which must stay with it.

## Run it locally

It is plain static files with no build step. From this folder:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then open http://localhost:8000/. Opening `index.html` straight from disk will not work, because browsers block module scripts and data requests on `file://` pages.

The page needs internet for the street basemap (tiles.openfreemap.org), for the elevation tiles (s3.amazonaws.com) behind Terrain shading and 3D terrain, which are on by default, and for each live layer (api.weather.gc.ca, geo.weather.gc.ca, earthquake.usgs.gov, api.gbif.org, api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov, and api-iwls.dfo-mpo.gc.ca for the Victoria Harbour gauge) once its box is ticked or its station is clicked. Seafloor depth draws its ETOPO1 colours from the same elevation tiles and, while its box is ticked, also asks nonna-geoserver.data.chs-shc.ca (the Canadian Hydrographic Service) for tiles. The eight Marine life and protection boxes make no request to any outside host either: each loads one file of this site when it is first ticked. If the basemap cannot be reached, the page shows the data layers on a plain background and adds a sentence to the banner. If a live service cannot be reached, its layer shows a plain failure line.

## What is on the map

Default on: the example place, creeks, lakes and wetlands (with Flow, below), parks and green spaces, terrain shading, 3D terrain, municipal boundaries. Default off: watersheds, ecosystem zones, seafloor depth, ecoregions, industry and waste sites, the eight marine life and protection layers, the seven layers under Change over time, and the twelve live layers. Clicking a feature opens a popup with its name, type and source. Popups are built from plain text only, and only https links are clickable.

**Flow.** A sub-toggle under "Creeks, lakes and wetlands", on by default. Pale dashes march downstream along the creek lines from zoom 9 up, so a visitor can see which way Bowker Creek, the Colquitz River or Craigflower Creek drain. It shows drainage direction from provincial lines, not discharge or current. Reaches the Province maps as main flow in a natural channel, and reaches it maps as canals, get dashes. Pipes (subsurface flow) and connection lines do not, and the panel and credits say so. Canals were added on 2026-10-21: Bowker Creek now shows about 1,420 m of its roughly 2,110 m mapped length (about 480 m of natural channel and 940 m of canal), where it showed about 480 m before, and the other 680 m, mapped as subsurface flow, stays undashed. Under a system setting of reduced motion the layer is hidden altogether, the box is off and disabled, and the page follows the setting if it changes. The animation loop runs only while the dashes can be seen: the Flow and creeks boxes are on, this tab is in front and the map is zoomed to 9 or more. How the direction was found and checked is in [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md).

*What a step costs.* The march is 14 steps, and each step shows a different one of 14 layers (`streams-flow`, then `streams-flow-1` to `streams-flow-13`), each with its own fixed dash array, by raising its opacity and dropping the opacity of the one before. The first version changed the dash array of one layer at every step, and MapLibre answered each change by rebuilding that layer's tiles in its worker, every time (measured 2026-10-23 in the desktop view: 36 to 48 tile reloads in 8 s, six for each step, and the same with a `global-state` expression instead). A change of opacity makes none. Either way a step redraws the whole map, and that redraw is what costs: in the headless software renderer, on a machine at a load of 60 to 170 on 8 cores, one took about 0.4 to 1.6 s at 1280 px and 3 to 7 s with 3D terrain. A phone's graphics chip is quicker, but it does the redraw at every step too. So after a step the loop waits for the frame that draws it, takes that frame's length as the cost of a redraw and rests for twice as long (up to 5 s, `flowRestMs` in [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js)), so the dashes never take more than a third of the time a device spends drawing. (The time MapLibre's own `render` event reports is only the time the page spent, not the time the graphics chip did, and the first try used it and did not slow down at all in the headless browser.) A step is chosen from the clock, so the dashes keep their pace on a device that keeps up and skip ahead on one that does not.

Measured on 2026-10-23 in headless Chromium 140 (software graphics) with Flow on, the map at zoom 12.5 over Bowker Creek, 8 s for each row, each pair taken one after the other, "before" being the code of the commit before this one. The machine was shared with other sessions, so the load average of the 8 cores (read at the start and the end of the run) is in each row, and the frame times say how busy the machine was more than what a phone would do. The steadier columns are the tile reloads and the CPU time of the browser's processes. With Flow off the page drew 60 frames a second and used 0.10 to 0.45 CPU seconds a second.

Before, every frame was a step and every step a redraw plus a rebuild of the creek tiles, so the page was always busy and a frame took as long as the redraw. After, the page answers at the screen's pace between steps and takes one slow frame for each redraw, the slowest being a 3D redraw on the loaded machine (3 to 8 s here, which a real graphics chip does not take). The redraws a second fell by 36% to 67%, the tile reloads to none, and the browser's CPU time fell by 45% to 75% for the desktop views and the phone view in 3D, and by 12% for the phone view flat, where it was already low. How fast a phone draws a step was not measured (see "Not tested yet"). A redraw costs no more with the 14 layers in than with the one before: with the steps stopped and the map redrawn at every frame, a redraw took 239 and 237 ms against 627 and 188 ms with one layer (desktop, flat) and 344 and 348 ms against 348 and 399 ms (desktop, 3D terrain), at loads of 59 to 99, so the 13 layers at opacity 0 add nothing measurable.

**Terrain shading.** A hillshade drawn in the browser from AWS Terrain Tiles. On by default (since 2026-10-21, at Darren's request). Credits and the on-map attribution name the sources while it is on. With both terrain boxes unticked, no elevation tile is requested.

**3D terrain.** On by default (since 2026-10-21, at Darren's request): the map opens flat and tilts into relief once it has loaded, with no animation when the device asks for reduced motion. The box lifts the map into relief with MapLibre's terrain support, from the same AWS Terrain Tiles (a second source on the same tile address, because MapLibre warns that one source shared with the hillshade lowers the quality of both). The exaggeration is 2 (`TERRAIN_EXAGGERATION` in [js/app.js](js/app.js), 1 is true height). The map tilts to 60 degrees (`TERRAIN_PITCH`), drag-rotate, two-finger tilt and the compass are switched on, and a sky and fog are added. Unticking it removes the terrain and puts the pitch and bearing back to 0. While it is off, the map is flat and north-up and rotation is locked. The Terrain shading box stays independent of it. The relief has no lighting of its own, so the note under the box says Terrain shading gives the hills their light and shade. The parks, creeks, watersheds and the other flat layers are draped over the relief by MapLibre, and the check queries them with 3D on. The Flow dashes step at half the rate while 3D terrain is on, because each step redraws the draped creeks. Performance was not measured on a phone, see "Not tested yet" below.

**Popups on a phone.** On a screen up to 640 px wide, or up to 500 px high (a phone held sideways), a popup is at most the width of the screen less 16 px (290 px at most) and at most 60% of its height, and no taller than the room between the banner and the bottom controls, and it scrolls inside itself. After it opens, the map is moved by the least that brings the whole popup inside 8 px margins, below the banner and above the attribution button (`keepInView` in [js/app.js](js/app.js), which the tide popups already used when their levels arrived). MapLibre alone chooses only which corner or side a popup hangs from, so a 290 px popup opened 120 px from the left edge of a 390 px screen ran 20 px past the right edge, one opened 270 px from it ran 20 px past the left edge, and a tall one opened low on the screen ran off the bottom. The browser check found all three on the code before the change (right edge at 410 px, left edge at -20 px and bottom at 868 px, on a screen 390 by 780) and finds none now, at 15 positions. On a wider screen nothing changes and the map is not moved. A popup opens at its top. MapLibre moves focus to the first link, which is at the end of a long popup, and the browser scrolls to it: Chrome at once, so setting the scroll to 0 afterwards worked there, and Safari later, so in WebKit a tall popup opened scrolled to its end (found 2026-10-23). The page now moves focus to the same link itself, without scrolling.

**Ecoregions.** Off by default. The RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017 polygon for the area, loaded from this site on the first tick. The whole demo box falls in one ecoregion, Puget lowland forests, and the shape is a coarse copy, so the layer is a label for the region more than a map. See [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md).

**Industry and waste sites.** Off by default. Eighteen sites in the box: 11 landfills and waste sites, 6 wastewater plants and pump stations and one power plant. Only records from sources whose licences were read as open are used. A dot with no figures is a location, not an empty site, and every figure says whether it was reported, is a capacity or is modelled.

**Change over time.** Seven boxes under "Change over time", all off by default. Six of them put their years on one time slider at the bottom of the map ([js/timeline.js](js/timeline.js)), so every ticked layer moves through time together. The slider shows only while one of them is on. It has Earlier and Later buttons, a range input with the year as its spoken value, and Play with a speed box (0.5x, 1x, 2x and 4x, starting at 1x), which moves one step at a time, waits for each step's pictures (at most 10 s), shows each step for 0.5 s at 1x (1 s at 0.5x, 0.125 s at 4x) and stops at the last step. With only two steps on the slider (the ecosystems or the air photos alone) the bar is Earlier and Later only, with no Play, speed or slider. A layer with a stored picture per year (land cover, tree canopy) fades from one year to the next over 0.25 s, at once under reduced motion. An air photo stays drawn until the next year's tiles are in, so the map is never empty between years. Play is disabled under reduced motion, with the reason shown, and does not step while the tab is hidden. The readout under the slider has one line per layer, in the order of the boxes, and says which year each layer is showing. A layer answers a year in one of three ways: an event layer shows only that year (herring spawn), a snapshot layer shows the latest picture made in or before it and says so ("Air photo: 1950, the latest before 1967"), and a cumulative layer shows everything up to it (forest loss). A layer just ticked starts the slider at its first step when the current year has nothing of it. The layers and their text are in [js/time-layers.js](js/time-layers.js) and [js/time-data.js](js/time-data.js).

- *Herring spawn by year, 1932 to 2026.* One circle per place and herring season (July to June), sized by Fisheries and Oceans Canada's spawn index in tonnes, over grey rings that mark every place with spawn in any season. 807 places and seasons in the area the map reaches. The popup gives the place as DFO names it, the season, the index and how many records it comes from, the shore length, the survey method and the caveats: a relative index and a minimum estimate, and surveys that changed in 1969, 1979 and 1988. Before 1951 the seasons come from a DFO report of 1989 that gives spawn by shore section, with no places: 21 seasons of Victoria Harbour, Saanich Inlet and one record in Sidney Channel (1932). Each is a large ring over the section's spawning area, kept the same size on the ground at every zoom. Its popup gives the records, the shore length, the dates, the intensity on the five categories used until 1968, that the ring is the section and not the place, and DFO's caveat that records before 1951 are unreliable for indexing.
- *Air photos, 1932 and 1950.* The National Air Photo Library mosaics of central Victoria, Oak Bay, Esquimalt and south Saanich, made into WebP tiles by [scripts/build-aerial-tiles.sh](scripts/build-aerial-tiles.sh) and read from the map's own server, map-api.regeneratevictoria.org, for the year shown (the year before stays drawn until the new year's tiles are in). The files are too big for this site, so they are copied to the server by hand (see Air photo tiles below). With no other layer on the slider, the photos get the swipe, as the ecosystems do: two maps of their own draw 1932 left of a line the visitor drags (or moves with the arrow keys) and 1950 right of it, and Later and Earlier snap the line to the edges. The two maps follow the page's map on every move, with the same terrain in 3D and the same height of the centre, so they line up. They only draw, and the page's map keeps every click. There is no open air photo of today, so the street map is the "now".
- *Garry oak and other sensitive ecosystems, 1994-95 and 2002.* The Sensitive Ecosystems Inventory polygons with woodland, herbaceous ground, coastal bluff or sparse vegetation. The 2002 update kept the 1990s polygons and marked what had been disturbed since 1992, drawn in red. The inventory has no Garry oak class, so the page says "woodland, including Garry oak". It is "mapped then and mapped later", not a measure of every change. With no other layer on the slider it has a swipe: a line with 1994-95 on its left and 2002 on its right, each drawn by a map of its own over the page's map (so neither year shows through the other), while the page's map keeps both at no opacity for clicks, and a click opens the year drawn on its side. Later snaps the line to the left edge, so 2002 fills the map, and Earlier to the right edge. A line between them names both years and lets both arrows work, and a line moved to an edge moves the year. The photos' swipe and this one are never open together.
- *Garry oak ecosystems mapped in 2014 (west only).* A later and finer mapping in Metchosin, Sooke and the land around them, with other methods. It is a box of its own and not a step of the slider.
- *Land cover, 2010, 2015 and 2020.* Natural Resources Canada's 30 m land cover from Landsat, in the demo box, as one stored picture of the three years (218 KB) that the page colours per year, so a step fades from one year to the next at once. A key lists the eleven classes in the box.
- *Tree canopy from LiDAR, Victoria, 2013 to 2023.* The City of Victoria's tree canopy layers of 2013, 2019 and 2023 (made by Terra Remote Sensing Inc. from LiDAR at 1 m), drawn in one colour where each has a value, on one grid of about 2 m for all three years. The values are not shown, because their unit is not stated anywhere found. The 2023 file is marked as a draft, and the slider says so. City of Victoria only. The three pictures are loaded once, when the box is ticked.
- *Forest loss, 2001 to 2025.* The University of Maryland's Global Forest Change loss year (Landsat, 30 m cells), coloured from pale yellow for 2001 to dark red for 2025. The slider shows the loss from 2001 up to its year. The data is one small picture (88 KB) in which each cell's red value is its year, and the page paints the cells up to the year itself, so a step changes only the cells between two years. Shapes made from the same grid were 2.9 MB. A click on a shown cell, or next to one, opens its year. Its makers say the years before 2011 were processed differently and that area should not be estimated from its cells, so the map gives no areas.

*Years after today and other pasts.* The control sorts steps by their year and shows their labels. It assumes nothing about today, so a later round can add scenario years after today as steps of their own, and another past (what if) as a second list of steps a layer registers in place of its first. Neither is built, and either needs a sourced model, not invented numbers.

**Live data.** Twelve layers, grouped under "Live data" in the panel, off by default. Nothing is requested from an outside service until its box is ticked, and the panel says so. The tide and current stations are the one exception to "on tick": ticking Tides and tidal currents shows the stations from a list in the page, and NOAA, or the Canadian Hydrographic Service for the Victoria Harbour gauge, is asked only when a station is clicked. Each shows a plain status line, and a plain failure line if its request fails (the alerts line also says that the map is then not showing whether any alert is in effect). Each is loaded straight from the visitor's browser, so there is no key and no server of ours in between.

*The GeoMet server.* Environment Canada's GeoMet server (geo.weather.gc.ca) serves the radar, sea currents, wind, sea temperature and sea saltiness. It is not steady: on 2026-10-22 its replies took from under a second to 12 s, and some requests timed out or were refused. So every request to it (`geometGet` in [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js)) has its own time limit of 15 s, which covers the reply to its last byte, and is tried once more after 1.5 s if the first try got no answer, lost the connection or was answered with HTTP 408, 425, 429, 500, 502, 503 or 504. A time limit whose timer runs more than a second late means the page was busy, not that the server was slow (a page drawing a 3D view in software went 15 s without handling anything on 2026-10-22, with the answers in), so the answer, which may be waiting to be handled, gets one second more before the try is cut off. A request the server refuses for its own sake (a missing layer, an hour out of range) or answers with the wrong kind of file (on 2026-10-23 at 01:14 PDT it answered every CIOPS-SalishSea coverage request with HTTP 200 and "Unable to access file. Corrupt, empty or missing file." while its description of the hours listed a new run, and the cause was not found) is not tried twice, and its failure line says the server sent an error. The status line says what is happening: after 6 s without an answer, "Environment Canada’s server is slow to answer." and the line it had before, before the second try "Environment Canada’s server did not answer. Trying once more.", and if both tries fail the failure line adds "Environment Canada’s server is not answering." or "Environment Canada’s server sent an error." A layer's requests together (the description of the hours, then the hour) may take 60 s before the page gives up. Unticking a box stops its requests and any second try at once. The static self-tests play a stand-in server through every one of these cases, and the browser check does the same with the real page.

**Sea currents.** Off by default, in the Live data group. Ticking it asks the GeoMet service which hours the model offers, then for the two velocity components (east and north, in metres per second) of the latest hour not after now. The box asked for is west -124.5, south 47.8, east -122.6, north 49.2 (everywhere the map can go, with a margin), asked with `scalefactor=0.5`, so the service sends every second cell of its 500 m grid as two GeoTIFF images of 117 x 155 cells (73,018 bytes each, 146 KB an hour, against 235 x 311 cells and 586 KB without it; measured 2026-10-22: 7,495 of the 18,135 cells are sea, and every cell of the half-size grid equals a cell of the full one, so it is subsampled, not averaged). [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js) reads them with a small GeoTIFF reader (one band of 32-bit floats, not compressed, little- or big-endian) and refuses anything else, and treats NaN, a value above 100 m/s and exactly zero in both components as no data (that is how the model marks land). [js/flow.js](js/flow.js), the particle engine shared with Wind, turns the grid into a velocity field in screen pixels on a grid of points every 7 px. It asks the map which place lies under a lattice of points every 28 px (`map.unproject`, so pitch, rotation and 3D terrain are followed) and turns the slope of longitude and latitude over the screen into the direction the water goes on the screen. Rows above the horizon of a steeply tilted view are left out, because `map.unproject` answers there with places behind the camera. About 4,000 particles (1,500 where the window is narrower than 700 px, fewer where less sea is in view) are dropped on sea points, carried along the field and drawn as short segments that fade into trails: darker, thicker and longer for faster water. The trails are blue, from a mid blue for slow water to deep navy above 0.8 m/s, because the basemap's sea is pale grey (#c2c8ca): the contrast of each band against it is 3.7 to 9.4 to 1, where the white and pale cyan used first had 1.2 to 1.9 to 1. A particle that reaches land, the edge of the model or the end of its life (1.5 to 3.5 s) starts again on the sea. The canvas is drawn over the map and under the popups and the panel, and lets every touch through. While the map moves the picture is cleared and the loop paused, and it starts again when the map stops. The loop also stops when the tab is hidden or the box is unticked, which removes the canvas. The pace on the screen is the same at every zoom: the velocity field is scaled by the typical pixels per metre of the view (`fieldScale` in [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js), the middle value over the field), so 0.5 m/s moves about 30 px a second (`pxPerMs` 60, `FLOW_PROFILES` in [js/flow.js](js/flow.js)) whether the view is the whole strait or a bay, measured in the browser check as 58.8 to 60.0 px a second for each m/s at zoom 8.6, 10.4 and 12.5. Tilt is kept, so far water still moves less on the screen than near water, and a particle never moves more than 10 px in a frame. The note under the box gives that pace and no single speed-up factor, because a factor would be false at other zooms (the picture is about 2,700 times faster than the real water at the opening zoom of 10.2, and about 400 times faster at zoom 13). The status line names the model hour in Pacific time ("Modelled surface currents for Oct 21, 10 p.m. PDT, Environment Canada’s CIOPS Salish Sea model (experimental, not for navigation)."). Play steps one hour every 1.5 s through the 13 hours from now (one tide cycle is a little over 12). It starts the flow changing at once and smoothly: the field is blended from the hour shown to the one after it over the 1.5 s of the step, as a vector (`fieldBlendAt` in [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js)), so water that turns round between two hours slows to a stop on the way and the particles do not jump each hour, and the status line names the hour the blend started from. The hour after is fetched at the start of the step, asked for once even if two parts of the page want it, and the screen field of the next hour is made once and kept for the step after. Each blend costs one more screen field (about 5 to 9 ms in the headless browser). Pause goes back to the hour named, with no blend left. It keeps up to 14 hours in memory so a second loop makes no requests. It does not start by itself. Under reduced motion nothing moves: each particle is drawn once as a short streak that fades in toward the way the water goes, longer for faster water, and Play is disabled with the reason given. The credit on the map is the Environment and Climate Change Canada Data Server End-use Licence statement, the same as the other Environment Canada layers, and the credits panel, the status line and the note call the model experimental and not for navigation. The picture is a drawing of a model, not a measurement. Clicking the sea while the layer is on opens a popup with the modelled current at that point for the hour shown: the speed in knots and metres per second, the compass point the water flows toward ("flowing toward the north-east", from eight points), the hour, and "Model estimate, not for navigation". It reads the grid already loaded, makes no request, and says "Almost still" with no direction below 0.03 m/s. Every other popup is unchanged: a click on a pin, park, creek or other feature opens that popup alone, and on water inside a municipal, ecosystem-zone or ecoregion area the current comes first and the area's own popup follows it. With the layer off, or with Wind on, a click on the sea shows no current.

**Wind.** Off by default, in the Live data group, next to Sea currents. It is the other half of what [earth.nullschool.net](https://earth.nullschool.net) shows, drawn with the same engine, over land and sea, in purple (deep plum above 9 m/s) so it is not mixed up with the blue currents. Ticking it asks the GeoMet service which hours the HRDPS layer `HRDPS.CONTINENTAL_WSPD` offers (2026-10-22: `2026-10-22T06:00:00Z/2026-10-24T06:00:00Z/PT1H`, 49 hours), then for the wind speed (`HRDPS.CONTINENTAL_WSPD`, metres per second, 10 m above the surface) and the wind direction (`HRDPS.CONTINENTAL_WD`, degrees) of the latest hour not after now, over the same box as the currents, asked as `scalesize=x(57),y(62)`: 57 x 62 cells of about 2.5 km, the model's own spacing, 14,562 bytes each, 29 KB an hour (measured). The service has no east and north wind layer for WCS (`HRDPS.CONTINENTAL_UU` answers "WCS request not enabled"), so [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js) makes the east and north components from the two (`windGrid`) before anything is interpolated, because directions cannot be averaged. The direction is the one the wind blows from, clockwise from north, checked on 2026-10-22 against 25 station reports across Canada with a speed of 15 km/h or more: the model direction was within a median of 9.6 degrees of the station when read as "from" and 170.4 degrees when read as "to" (see [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md)), so a wind from the west (270) blows east and the particles move that way, which the static self-test checks for all four compass directions and north-east. The speed is in metres per second, not knots or km/h (the layer title says so, a feature query gave 3.35 m/s for the class "5 - 10 kts", and the stations' km/h over the model's m/s came out at about 4.5, not 1.9). Every cell with a number is data, and exactly calm is data too, because wind blows over land. The particles move at 12 px a second for each m/s (5 m/s is about 60 px a second) at every zoom. There are about 4,000 on a wide screen and 1,500 on a narrow one. Sea currents and Wind cannot both be on: ticking one unticks the other, because there is one canvas and one engine, and the one that goes off drops its hours from memory. The status line names the hour and the model ("Modelled wind for Oct 22, 4 a.m. PDT, Environment Canada’s HRDPS model (forecast, not for navigation)."). Play, Pause, the hours kept, reduced motion (still streaks, Play disabled) and the credit work as for Sea currents. Wind has no click readout.

**Night look.** Off by default, one box at the top of the panel ("Night look"). It turns the basemap dark and the labels light, and the trails of Sea currents and Wind into pale, thin lines that last longer, like the look of [earth.nullschool.net](https://earth.nullschool.net). OpenFreeMap also serves a dark style of its own (`https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/dark`, found 2026-10-22 and not in its documentation, which lists three styles), and it was not used: it is undocumented, it would need every layer of the page added again after a style swap, and its labels are grey on black. Instead the colours of the style that is loaded are darkened in the browser ([js/night.js](js/night.js), `nightColour` in [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js)), with no request: the ground is (30, 34, 40) and the sea (13, 31, 50), text becomes light and its halo dark, and every other colour keeps its look turned about the ground (what was darker than the land becomes lighter than the ground by about as much, a white road a little lighter still), with its hue kept and its chroma cut. The first time the box is ticked the colour of each layer is kept, and unticking puts every one back exactly (the browser check compares them). Road shields are left alone, because their black text sits on a white picture. The lakes, the wetlands, the hillshade and the municipal boundaries, which were drawn for a pale map, get colours of their own, and the 3D sky is dark. The trails are pale blue-white for the currents and pale lavender-white for the wind, from a contrast of 7 to 1 or more against the sea for the slowest to white for the fastest, 1 to 1.8 px wide, and 47% of a trail is still there a second later (25% by day, where the trails are dark and last about half a second), so it lasts about a second (nullschool fades its trail 3% each 40 ms frame, which is 47% a second). The Sea temperature and Sea saltiness picture is drawn at 0.8 opacity over the dark sea, where a colour needs more paint to stand out. The Canadian Hydrographic Service tiles carry the colour of the water, so they are asked for and painted again when the look changes while Seafloor depth is on (the CHS server is asked again, as it is for every new view). Popups stay light, as the panel follows the device's own light or dark setting. The static self-tests check the colours (labels 7 to 1 or better against the ground, 4.5 to 1 for the faintest greys of the pale style) and the browser check switches the look on and off against the real page, with the currents, the wind, the temperature, Seafloor depth and 3D terrain.

**Ideas from earth.nullschool.net.** The look of Sea currents, Wind and the two sea layers borrows these ideas from [earth.nullschool.net](https://earth.nullschool.net) (code at https://github.com/cambecc/earth, MIT licence, Copyright (c) 2014 Cameron Beccario). They were read in `products.js`, `earth.js` and `micro.js` on 2026-10-22. No code and no colour table of it is copied, so the code carries no notice, and the ideas are named here instead:

- Particles are thin one-pixel lines drawn on a canvas over the map.
- A trail is made by fading the canvas a few percent each frame (3% each 40 ms frame there), so it lasts about a second.
- The brightness of a trail follows the speed.
- A particle ages and starts again somewhere else (100 frames of 40 ms there).
- The particle count follows the size of the window.
- A colour overlay is made by interpolating the data to the pixels of the screen and painting a smooth scale over it, not one flat colour for each cell, and it is left part transparent (40% there) so the map shows through.
- A colour scale is a smooth run between chosen colours. Its temperature scale runs from -80 to 55 degrees Celsius, so the ramps here are made for the Salish Sea's range instead.
- The look is pale on dark.

The blend of Play between two forecast hours is not from it. The source read there interpolates only in space (bilinearly between grid points) and steps from one forecast layer to the next when the visitor navigates, so the smooth Play is this map's own. The ramps, the Night colours, the blend as a vector, the pace on the screen and the bands are this map's own too.

**Sea temperature and Sea saltiness.** Off by default, in the Live data group, next to Wind. The temperature layer is in kelvin and the page turns it into degrees Celsius. *Colours.* The service's own pictures were used first, and they were replaced because their colour styles run over about 30 degrees, so the 9 to 13 degree sea of 22 October came out as one band of turquoise to green (the best of the service's styles had 25 colours across the 5th to 95th percentile and no other had the Salish Sea's range, see [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md)). Now each pixel is coloured from the number at its place, by a ramp made for this sea and the same for every hour: 6 to 20 degrees Celsius (blue, teal, green, yellow, orange, red) and 20 to 34 psu (pale sand for fresh water through green and teal to blue, indigo and deep violet for the saltiest, darker with every step). The ramps run through eight chosen colours with a smooth curve in CIELAB, so equal steps look about equally different, and a number beyond the ends shows as the end colour (the readout still gives the number). On 2026-10-22 the sea held 9.4 to 13.2 degrees and 12.6 to 32.3 psu, and the ramps leave room on both sides for the seasons. Each whole degree from 9 to 13 is 20 units of CIELAB from the next, where it was one band of colour before. The picture is made from the grid in the browser (`scalarPicture` in [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js)): 480 x 511 pixels in web Mercator over the area of the radar picture, each pixel the bilinear value between the four cells round it that are sea, and the coast feathered over half a cell so it is not a staircase of 1 km cells (about 20 ms on the machine used for the checks). The model marks land as no data, so the picture covers only sea (about 47% of it). It sits above the basemap's sea and the sea masks of the ecosystem zones and ecoregions, and under the creeks, parks, boundaries and labels, at 0.65 opacity. Measured in the browser check with the picture and the basemap's water at the same places, under 10% of the picture lies over basemap land (6% in the view tried, because the model's cells are 500 m) and nearly all the water in view is covered. The particles of Sea currents are drawn on a canvas over the map, so they stay above the picture.

*The key and the readout.* The key under each box is the ramp itself, a gradient with a stop every half degree or psu (29), computed from the same function that colours the picture, not measured from a picture. The static self-tests check that the stops are the ramp's colours, and the browser check that the picture's pixels at 8 places across the sea are the ramp's colour for the number the page reads there (within 2 of 255 in each channel: the largest difference was 1 for the temperature and 0 for the saltiness). Clicking the sea while one is on adds a line to the popup, "Surface sea temperature: 11.5 °C (for Oct 22, 5 a.m. PDT)." or the saltiness in psu, read from the grid already loaded: the readout makes no request, and it is the number the colour stands for. With Sea currents on, it comes under the current in the same popup. With only a temperature or saltiness picture on, a click on the basemap's water opens its own popup. Land, and water the model has no cell for, give a plain line and no number. The status line, the credits and the readout say experimental, model estimate and not for navigation. The credit on the map is the Environment and Climate Change Canada statement, as for the other layers from that service.

**Seafloor depth.** Off by default, in the Water group. The sea coloured by depth in metres, with one key, from two sources. *ETOPO1.* The colours run from a pale blue at 25 m to a dark blue at 300 m, fade in from 10 m and are transparent from there up to the waterline and above, which leaves land alone. - **Protected marine areas.** The marine zones of protected areas in the Canadian Protected and Conserved Areas Database (CPCAD, Open Government Licence - Canada). CPCAD splits each area into a marine zone and a terrestrial zone, and the rule for what is drawn is that field: marine zones only, so the land of a park is not drawn twice (it is on the map already from the provincial and local park files). 39 marine zones touch the area the map reaches and 22 are in the file, among them Race Rocks, the Oak Bay Islands, Satellite Channel and Ten Mile Point ecological reserves, the Victoria Harbour, Esquimalt Lagoon and Shoal Harbour migratory bird sanctuaries, the Gulf Islands National Park Reserve, Gowlland Tod and Goldstream (marine zones), Montague Harbour Marine Park and the Outer Gulf Islands Glass Sponge Marine Refuge. Discovery Island Marine Park has no marine zone in CPCAD, so it shows only as the park it already is. A popup gives the name, type, owner, year of establishment and, where CPCAD gives one, the no-take line. That line is CPCAD's "Marine Resource Use" field (`NO_TAKE`), worded as CPCAD's own documentation defines it (its 2025 user manual and data dictionary, quoted in [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md)): "No-take, as reported to CPCAD: all of the area", "part of the area" or "none of the area", and for all and part also "CPCAD's meaning of no-take: the taking of living or dead natural resources, inclusive of all methods of fishing, extraction, dumping, dredging and construction, is strictly prohibited." The documentation's codes for "not reported" ("It is not known whether there is a no-take area.") and "not applicable" are not said, so the three migratory bird sanctuaries, which read not reported, show no no-take line. The four ecological reserves read All.
- **Southern Resident killer whale critical habitat.** One polygon from Fisheries and Oceans Canada's Species at Risk critical habitat (Open Government Licence - Canada), status final or proposed. The record says its map is "for general guidance only", and the popup says that, says it is a boundary and not a sighting, and gives the vessel distances below. It is drawn as a dashed outline with a faint fill.
- **Killer whale important area.** One polygon of 775 km2 from the Fisheries and Oceans Canada layer "Resident killer whale" of Important Areas for the Strait of Georgia Ecoregion (Open Government Licence - Canada, read on its catalogue record on 2026-10-23). Experts drew it by hand on paper maps in 2009 as important to Southern Resident killer whales from June to October, and rated its importance high. It is a mapped area, not a sighting: its record says how it was made and that the areas "have no enhanced management in place at this time" and are "Not for navigational purposes". The popup says all of that, gives the vessel distances and does not name the experts (the record names one person in its text and two in the feature, see [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md)). It is drawn as a dotted dark red outline with the same faint fill as the critical habitat, because it overlaps the critical habitat (about 38% of the vertices of its outer edge are inside it) without lying inside it. The file is the one polygon with two fields, simplified to 8% (31,957 bytes), and the check stops if it has another season, another score or any other field. Added in this round because the density model has no killer whales.
- **Whale and porpoise density (model).** The Salish Sea grid of the Fisheries and Oceans Canada PRISMM density models (Open Government Licence - Canada): 223 cells of at most 25 km2 for the humpback whale, the harbour porpoise and Dall's porpoise, picked in a list under the box. They are modelled from ship surveys between July and early September 2018, and the panel, the popup and the credits say so and say they are not where animals are now. The colour is the model's number for the cell divided by the cell's area, in animals per 100 km2, in five classes of equal share of the cells for the species picked (the key gives the four limits, for example 0.85, 1.2, 1.6 and 2.8 for the humpback whale). A popup gives the species, the model number as the service names it (Nhat, the animals predicted in the cell) with the cell's area, the density, the uncertainty (CV) and the survey months and year. The cells are Canadian waters only, so none is drawn on the American side of the border. The three species share one file of cells, joined by `scripts/make-density.mjs`, which stops if the three layers ever differ in a cell.
- **Kelp beds, Eelgrass, Herring spawn and Seabird colonies.** Four files from the Province's Coastal Resource Information Management System (CRIMS, Open Government Licence - British Columbia), each its own box under one line that says CRIMS is a legacy survey that began in 1979 and was only updated now and then, so it is not a current record (the record says the Province has no plans to update it). 1,118 kelp beds with species and density where the survey gives them, 22 eelgrass beds (all mapped in 1998), 581 herring spawn shores (records from 1937 to 1986) and 81 seabird colonies by name. Every popup says the survey is old. A seabird colony popup says nesting birds are easily disturbed and to keep well back, and names no species, because the survey's species codes have no published dictionary. One colony named for a First Nation is left out by name, as the one industry site was.
- **Vessel distances.** Wherever whales appear, the page gives these distances, from Transport Canada's Ship Safety Bulletin 08/2026 (the 1,000 m rule) and the Marine Mammal Regulations, Schedule VI (the 200 m and 100 m rules), both read on 2026-10-22: "Vessels must stay at least 1,000 m from Southern Resident killer whales from June 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027, at least 200 m from all other killer whales and at least 100 m from all other whales, dolphins and porpoises. Some exceptions apply." The sentence is one constant in [js/overlays-data.js](js/overlays-data.js), and the panel notes, the popups and the credits carry it, with links to both sources. The bulletin lists exceptions (for example vessels in transit), so the sentence says some apply.

### Ask the map (built, not switched on)

A chat with an AI model that answers from this map's layers. [js/chat.js](js/chat.js) sends the conversation and the map's state (view, year, ticked layers) to the chat service at map-api.regeneratevictoria.org/chat ([server/chat-service.mjs](server/chat-service.mjs)), which holds the model key, fixes the instructions, the model and the tools, accepts only this map's page, limits requests per address and per day, keeps nothing, and passes the conversation to the model: here Gemma on TELUS's AI platform in Quebec, with `MODEL_STREAM=0`, because that service's streamed tool calls garble nested arguments. The service also works with Groq (whose Zero Data Retention setting must then be on in the account) or OpenRouter, and with a second endpoint (`FALLBACK_URL`, `FALLBACK_KEY`, `FALLBACK_MODEL`) that takes a question at once when the first answers 429 or 413. Through OpenRouter instead (`MODEL_URL` left at its default), it asks only for providers that do not keep or train on prompts. The model can ask for six map commands (turn a layer on or off, outline features, move the map, set the year, open a feature, clear) and two reads (what a layer is, and up to 50 of its features), which this page runs. [data/manifest.json](data/manifest.json) lists each layer once: what it is and is not, its years, the fields a question may use, its record and licence, and whether the model may read it. Live layers, anything from the Canadian Hydrographic Service and layers with no stated licence are never read: the model may only turn them on. Answers cite layers and features, and the page turns each citation into a chip whose title, licence and link come from the manifest, never from the model. The service needs a key before it can answer, so the chat says it is off until then.

## Left out on purpose

None of these is in the demo, and none is linked.

- **Native Land territories, languages and treaties.** Its Data Sovereignty Treaty (https://api-docs.native-land.ca/data-sovereignty-treaty) lists "Store or distribute API data without explicit permission from Native Land Digital." as prohibited, so a copy of the polygons in this repository is out. Every API request needs a key tied to one person's agreement, and a static page cannot keep a key secret. Native Land Digital's own pages disagree (one says the data can be treated as CC0, the Treaty and FAQ say otherwise), so the stricter text governs. It needs Native Land Digital's written permission first. See open item A2.
- **RUSH rain garden map data and any link to RUSH.** Excluded until RUSH agrees.
- **HydroSHEDS HydroBASINS and HydroRIVERS.** The rights review is not recorded as cleared. The rivers file is 73 MB and 500 m resolution. The Freshwater Atlas replaces both.
- **Records, graph and people from the [Salish Sea Atlas](https://salishsee.life) project, and Indigenous place names.** Publishing Indigenous place names needs the Nations' permission, which has not been sought. The Atlas's industry and waste snapshot, ecoregion tiles and its live-layer endpoints were used as sources of data and methods, not its code.
- **Industry and waste sites from sources with unclear or share-alike licences.** The Atlas snapshot has 995 sites in the Salish Sea. Of the 29 in the demo box, 18 are shown. Dropped: 10 modelled river-plastic outfalls (points with no name, from a model, not sites), one wastewater plant named for a First Nation (this demo shows no First Nations content and no Nation has been asked, so the name is left out until someone decides), and every record from Climate TRACE (its terms list external datasets with other terms), IMF PortWatch (IMF terms) and Maus et al. mining footprints (CC BY-SA). Where a site has an open record and a Climate TRACE record, only the open record is used.
- **A Natural Resources Canada earthquake feed.** Looked for on 2026-10-21 and not found in a form a browser can read: the Earthquakes Canada site answered HTML with no Access-Control-Allow-Origin header, and no JSON or GeoJSON address was found. The U.S. Geological Survey catalogue is the only earthquake source.
- **Canadian Hydrographic Service data beyond two uses.** The owner accepted the CHS licences on 2026-10-22 for the NONNA seafloor tiles and the Victoria Harbour tide gauge (IWLS) only, both asked for live from the visitor's browser and never stored on this site (see Seafloor depth and Tides and tidal currents; the visitor's browser may cache a seafloor tile for a day). - **Whale sightings, whale-watch boats and ship tracking.** No live or recent sighting position and no AIS vessel position is shown anywhere: that would send boats to whales. ## Adding pins

Edit [data/pins.geojson](data/pins.geojson) on GitHub or in a pull request. A pin appears only if `status` is `approved`. No invented events: a pin is added only once its organiser has confirmed it. Every property is plain text.

| Property | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id` | short unique name |
| `kind` | `happening`, `learning-journey`, `partner-event` or `example-place` |
| `title` | shown in the popup and as the map label |
| `summary` | plain text, 300 characters or fewer |
| `date` | text, optional |
| `place_name` | text, optional |
| `url`, `url_label` | an https link and its text (the organiser's own page for partner events) |
| `source_url` | an https link to the source of the facts |
| `status` | `draft` or `approved` |

The geometry is a `Point` with `[longitude, latitude]`. For events at private homes use a public place or round to about 100 m. Do not add private addresses or named private individuals without their consent. The popup tag and the empty-state text follow `kind`, so adding a real event needs no code change. The Layers panel label still reads "Example place" and counts every pin, so rename it when real events go in. While no real pins exist, the Layers panel says "No events listed yet."

If pins later come from a Google Sheet, publish only a separate tab that holds approved rows. "Publish to the web" exposes every row of the published tab, so a status column alone does not keep drafts private. CORS on a published sheet has not been tested.

## Checks

```sh
node scripts/check-demo.mjs            # static checks, plain Node, no dependencies
node scripts/check-demo.mjs --browser  # also runs the headless browser checks
```

[scripts/check-demo.mjs](scripts/check-demo.mjs) first proves each "must not contain" rule can fire, by running it on seeded bad input, then checks this folder: no held-layer file names (native-land, rush, hydro, person), no email addresses, phone numbers, tokens or private path markers in the page, data, README and fetch script (not in LICENSE, the other scripts or the git history), every URL on an allowed host, no root-relative paths, pin rules (status, kind, https links), a size limit of 10 MB, every layer file present with valid GeoJSON inside the demo box, the licence statement, banner and credits present, and the vendored MapLibre files matching their pinned checksums. It also checks that LICENSE is the MIT text, that the README says MIT covers the code only, that the park layer has no Capital Regional District rows, that the credits carry each kept provider's statement (Victoria, Saanich, Islands Trust) and the terrain lines word for word, and that the flow lines point downstream. The direction rule runs at two creek mouths, the Colquitz River at Portage Inlet and Bowker Creek at the Oak Bay shore, and its self-test feeds it reversed lines and a missing creek. A second self-test runs `scripts/make-flow.mjs` on a creek drawn the right way (it turns it around) and on one already drawn downstream (it refuses). For the new layers it checks that the flow file includes the canals and not the pipes (Bowker Creek open channel between 1,300 and 1,600 m, with self-tests for canals missing and pipes included), that every new layer has a box and all are off, including 3D terrain, that the Live data sentence is present, that Environment and Climate Change Canada data is never credited to the Open Government Licence, that the privacy note names every host the page fetches from after a box is ticked, that the credits carry the Environment Canada, USGS, ecoregion and industry source statements word for word, and that the industry file holds only the open sources, only the kinds shown and no site named for a First Nation. ## Refreshing the data

[scripts/fetch-layers.sh](scripts/fetch-layers.sh) downloads the layers from the BC Data Catalogue WFS and rebuilds `data/*.geojson` with mapshaper 0.7.93 and, for the flow lines, [scripts/make-flow.mjs](scripts/make-flow.mjs) (plain Node). Its last section rebuilds the eight marine files from the CPCAD, Fisheries and Oceans Canada and CRIMS services (and [scripts/make-density.mjs](scripts/make-density.mjs) for the density cells), and `ONLY=marine sh scripts/fetch-layers.sh` runs just that section and leaves every other data file as it is (it was run that way on 2026-10-22, once with the downloads and again with `FETCH=0` to rebuild from them). Run it on a developer machine, never from the browser, then run the checks and commit. Set `FETCH=0` to reprocess already-downloaded files in `raw/` (which is not committed). On 2026-10-21 the whole script, download step included, was run from scratch. It reproduced seven of the committed layers byte for byte, changed `greenspaces.geojson` (Capital Regional District rows removed) and created `streams-flow.geojson`. The Islands Trust row was filtered out of the committed file for a time and put back on 2026-10-21 from the earlier local build of the file. Because the simplifier ranks vertices across the whole layer, a full run can differ from the committed `greenspaces.geojson` by a few vertices in that row and in other parks.

[scripts/fetch-time-layers.sh](scripts/fetch-time-layers.sh) rebuilds the herring spawn by year (with [scripts/make-herring-years.mjs](scripts/make-herring-years.mjs)) and the three Sensitive Ecosystems Inventory files, and [scripts/fetch-time-rasters.sh](scripts/fetch-time-rasters.sh) the three vegetation pictures and the forest loss shapes (it needs GDAL). `FETCH=0` reprocesses the downloads in `raw/`. The herring and inventory files were rebuilt from the downloads of 2026-10-22 on 2026-10-25 and came out byte for byte the same.

[scripts/make-industry.mjs](scripts/make-industry.mjs) builds `data/industry-waste.geojson` from the Salish Sea Atlas snapshot `material-sites-salish.json` (pass its path as the first argument). It keeps sites inside the box from the three open sources, drops every other source's records, the river-plastic kind and the site named for a First Nation, and keeps each figure's basis in words. `data/ecoregions.geojson` was made once, from the Atlas's `ecoregions-2017.pmtiles` (`pmtiles extract` for the box, then the z8 tile decoded to GeoJSON and clipped with mapshaper 0.7.93), and checked against the RESOLVE UTFGrid tiles at four points. The steps are in [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md).

## Hosting

The site is served by GitHub Pages, deployed from the `main` branch, root folder, at https://darrenzal.github.io/greater-victoria-map/. It is plain static files, so there is no build step. Every path in the page is relative, so it works under the project path. `.nojekyll` stops GitHub from running Jekyll over the files.

After each deploy:

1. Run `curl -sI -H 'Accept-Encoding: gzip'` on the three `.mjs` files under `vendor/` (`maplibre-gl.mjs`, `maplibre-gl-shared.mjs`, `maplibre-gl-worker.mjs`) and expect `content-type: text/javascript` and `content-encoding: gzip`. If `.mjs` were served with the wrong type, load MapLibre from the jsDelivr copy of 6.9.0 instead.
2. Open the page on a phone and a desktop, confirm the demo banner and attribution show, and open the example popup.

*Air photo tiles.* The two PMTiles files (about 17 MB and 97 MB) are too big for this site, so they are kept on Darren's server and served by Caddy at https://map-api.regeneratevictoria.org/tiles/, with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` and byte ranges. To change them, run `scripts/build-aerial-tiles.sh`, copy `raw/aerials/aerial-1932.pmtiles`, `aerial-1950.pmtiles` and `SHA256SUMS` to the server's tile folder, and check them there with `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS`. With `TILES_FROM=raw/aerials`, the browser check reads the local files instead of the server.

The repository is public, so its history is public too. Before pushing, run both checks and scan the repository for personal paths, email addresses and tokens. Updates: edit pins in the repository, or refresh the provincial data with the fetch script.

Visitor privacy: the page uses no analytics and no cookies. Map tiles come from tiles.openfreemap.org, so that service receives visitors' tile requests. Terrain shading and 3D terrain are on by default, so elevation tiles come from s3.amazonaws.com on every visit until a visitor switches both off, and Amazon Web Services receives those requests too. The credits say so. If a visitor ticks a live layer, their browser, with its internet address, contacts the service behind it: Environment and Climate Change Canada (api.weather.gc.ca for alerts, air quality, conditions and gauges, geo.weather.gc.ca for radar, sea currents, wind, sea temperature and sea saltiness), the U.S. Geological Survey (earthquake.usgs.gov), GBIF (api.gbif.org) or, when a station of Tides and tidal currents is clicked, the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov) or, for Victoria Harbour, the Canadian Hydrographic Service (api-iwls.dfo-mpo.gc.ca), or, while Seafloor depth is ticked, the Canadian Hydrographic Service's tile server (nonna-geoserver.data.chs-shc.ca), or, while Air photos is ticked, the map's own tile server (map-api.regeneratevictoria.org, Darren's server poly at Hetzner in Finland, which answers with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`). The privacy note in the credits names each of these hosts. All of them answer browsers directly (Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, checked with curl and an Origin header on 2026-10-21, for NOAA and the two CHS hosts on 2026-10-22, and by the requests the browser check makes from a page on another origin), so no proxy or key is needed. The IWLS service sets a load-balancer cookie on its replies (the tile server does not). A page's fetch does not send or keep cookies for another site, so none is stored in the visitor's browser. The tile notes say the elevation bucket is meant for efficient use from EC2 and is not behind a CDN, so a busy public site should host pre-extracted tiles instead. That matters more now that terrain is on by default. A self-hosted Protomaps extract of the Greater Victoria area (about 3.6 MB at zoom 13) would remove that third-party request, but needs hosted fonts and sprites and is about a day of work.

Risks: OpenFreeMap is a single-operator service with no SLA and can change or stop without notice, the boundaries are provincial and approximate, and a public repository makes the data files and the pins history public. The live layers add more of the same: each depends on a service that can change or stop, GBIF refuses a burst of requests (the loader asks one at a time and reports a partial result), and a busy public site would send many visitors' requests straight to Environment Canada, the USGS, GBIF, NOAA and the Canadian Hydrographic Service (one grid of 73 KB a visitor for each hour of Sea temperature or Sea saltiness, one or two for each tide station opened, three for the Victoria Harbour gauge, which the service limits to 3 a second and 30 a minute for each client, and a few dozen tiles of 16 to 43 KB each for each view of Seafloor depth, from a server whose uncached tiles can take several seconds). Environment Canada's usage policy was read on 2026-10-22 and is quoted in [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md). The USGS and GBIF terms were not read beyond the licences quoted there, and GBIF's own terms page could not be fetched from the live site (see open item A6).

- **Data edge.** The data layers end at the demo box, except the eight marine files, which end at the edge of the area the map reaches. On a wide screen the home view shows the western edge of the data near Sooke (about 123.75 W), where layers stop and the basemap continues. A wider box costs file size.
- **Creek length.** The Freshwater Atlas has 12 named Bowker Creek segments, about 2.1 km of a creek the 2012 Blueprint measures at 9.4 km, because piped stretches are unnamed or unmapped. The popups say so.
- **Watershed sizes.** The Province draws the Bowker Creek watershed at 1,381 ha. Other sources use other figures. The watershed popup says other groups draw watersheds differently.
- **Dependency on a newer MapLibre.** MapLibre 6.9.0 is pinned because it is the version already in use and tested in the related [Salish Sea Atlas](https://salishsee.life) project. Later releases exist, and an upgrade is a later, deliberate step. Version 6.x has no UMD file, so the page loads MapLibre as an ES module.
