Surface water, °C

Water 0.5 m down, coloured here from the model’s numbers on one scale from 6 to 20 °C, so a colour means the same at every hour. Warmer or colder shows as the end colour. Click the sea for the number. With Sea currents on, it shows the same hour and follows its Play. Sea temperature and Sea saltiness share one picture, so only one can be on.

Surface water, psu

Salt in the water 0.5 m down, in practical salinity units (psu, roughly grams of salt in a kilogram of water), coloured here from the model’s numbers on one scale from 20 to 34 psu. Fresher than 20 shows as the palest colour and saltier than 34 as the darkest. Click the sea for the number. With Sea currents on, it shows the same hour and follows its Play. Sea temperature and Sea saltiness share one picture, so only one can be on.

Teal dots are water level stations and violet dots are tidal current stations. Click one for the next tides or currents and a 24-hour curve. Victoria Harbour also shows what its gauge has measured.

Sea currents (off until you switch it on). Modelled surface currents, hour by hour, from the Coastal Ice Ocean Prediction System for the Salish Sea (CIOPS-SalishSea) of Environment and Climate Change Canada: a 500 m grid, a 48-hour forecast, tides included, served as an experimental product through its GeoMet service. The moving particles are a drawing of the model's currents, not a measurement, and they are sped up far beyond the real water so you can see them. This is an experimental model and is not for navigation or any safety decision. Each hour is two small images (about 146 KB in all, on a 1 km grid), requested only for the hour shown and, while Play runs, the next one. Clicking the sea while this layer is on shows the modelled current at that point, read from the images already loaded.

Wind (off until you switch it on). Modelled wind 10 m above the surface, hour by hour, from the High Resolution Deterministic Prediction System (HRDPS, 2.5 km) of Environment and Climate Change Canada, served through its GeoMet service. The moving particles are a drawing of the forecast, not a measurement, and they are sped up far beyond the real air so you can see them. It is a forecast model and is not for navigation or any safety decision: use Environment Canada's own marine and weather forecasts. Each hour is two small images (about 29 KB in all), requested only for the hour shown and, while Play runs, the next one. Wind and Sea currents share one picture, so only one can be on.

Sea temperature and Sea saltiness (each off until you switch it on). The temperature and the salt content of the water 0.5 m below the surface, hour by hour, from the Coastal Ice Ocean Prediction System for the Salish Sea (CIOPS-SalishSea) of Environment and Climate Change Canada, served as numbers through its GeoMet service. It is an experimental model on a 500 m grid, and the service is asked for every second cell, a 1 km grid. This map colours the numbers itself, on one scale for every hour (6 to 20 °C, 20 to 34 psu), and the key under each box is that scale. The picture is a drawing of the model, not a measurement, and it is not for navigation or any safety decision. Each hour is one grid of 73 KB, requested only for the hour shown (the hour of Sea currents while that is on, and while it plays, the next one). Clicking the sea while one is on reads the value from the grid already loaded and asks the service for nothing. Sea temperature and Sea saltiness share one picture, so only one can be on.

Tides and tidal currents (off until you switch it on). Predictions from the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA/NOS/CO-OPS), whose disclaimer says its information on government servers is “in the public domain” and asks for attribution. They are shown for two water level stations (Port Angeles and Friday Harbor) and five tidal current stations (Race Rocks, Discovery Island, Haro Strait, Trial Island and Cattle Point). A third water level station, Victoria Harbour, comes from the Canadian Hydrographic Service (next paragraph). The curves are drawn here from NOAA’s numbers and are not official NOAA material. Predictions are not for navigation. Nothing is requested from NOAA until you click a station. Then one or two small requests (about 1 to 9 KB in all) are made for that station, and they are not repeated for six hours.

Victoria Harbour tide gauge (part of Tides and tidal currents). Observed and predicted water levels from station 07120 of the Integrated Water Level System of the Canadian Hydrographic Service (CHS), used under the CHS licence agreement (dated 2023-08-14, read 2026-10-22). This is a non-commercial demo. The licence bars derivative products made for commercial purposes, for sale or profit, and this map is not made for any of them. The levels are not for navigation and none is stored on this site. Nothing is requested from CHS until you click the dot. Marine life and protection (each box off until you switch it on). These layers show protected areas, two mapped boundaries, a model and an old survey. None shows where an animal is now, and none is a sighting. Not for navigation. Each loads one file from this site when you tick it. The files were built on 2026-10-22 from open government data and cut at the edge of the area this map reaches.

Protected marine areas. From the Canadian Protected and Conserved Areas Database (CPCAD), compiled by Environment and Climate Change Canada from the governments and agencies that report each area. Only the marine zone of each area is shown, because the land of a park is on this map already. Race Rocks, the Oak Bay Islands, Satellite Channel and Ten Mile Point ecological reserves, the Victoria Harbour, Esquimalt Lagoon and Shoal Harbour migratory bird sanctuaries, the Gulf Islands National Park Reserve and the Outer Gulf Islands Glass Sponge Marine Refuge are among them. The no-take line in a popup is CPCAD’s own “Marine Resource Use” field (all, part or none of the area), with the meaning given in its user manual. Boundaries are approximate.

Southern Resident killer whale critical habitat. The boundary that Fisheries and Oceans Canada maps as critical habitat for this population under the Species at Risk Act (Critical Habitat for Aquatic Species at Risk – Canada), which says the map is “for general guidance only”. It is a boundary, not a sighting. Vessels must stay at least 1,000 m from Southern Resident killer whales from June 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027, at least 200 m from all other killer whales and at least 100 m from all other whales, dolphins and porpoises. Some exceptions apply. Rules: Transport Canada Ship Safety Bulletin 08/2026 for the 1,000 m distance and Marine Mammal Regulations, Schedule VI for the 200 m and 100 m distances.

Killer whale important area. One area that experts drew by hand on paper maps in 2009 as important to Southern Resident killer whales from June to October, from the Fisheries and Oceans Canada layer Important Areas for Cetaceans in Strait of Georgia Ecoregion. It is a boundary, not a sighting. Its edge is approximate, it carries no added management, and it does not show where the whales are now. Vessels must stay at least 1,000 m from Southern Resident killer whales from June 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027, at least 200 m from all other killer whales and at least 100 m from all other whales, dolphins and porpoises. Some exceptions apply. Rules: Transport Canada Ship Safety Bulletin 08/2026 for the 1,000 m distance and Marine Mammal Regulations, Schedule VI for the 200 m and 100 m distances.

Whale and porpoise density. A model from Fisheries and Oceans Canada (Spatial density models of cetaceans in the Canadian Pacific estimated from 2018 ship-based surveys), made from the Pacific Region International Survey of Marine Megafauna, which ran from July to early September 2018. It covers humpback whales, harbour porpoises and Dall’s porpoises in squares of 25 km² in Canadian waters only. It is a model of summer 2018, not where animals are now. The colours split the squares into five groups of equal size for the species picked, and the key gives animals per 100 km². A popup gives the model’s own number for the square (Nhat, the animals it predicts there) and its uncertainty (CV, higher is less certain). Vessels must stay at least 1,000 m from Southern Resident killer whales from June 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027, at least 200 m from all other killer whales and at least 100 m from all other whales, dolphins and porpoises. Some exceptions apply.

Change over time (each box off until you switch it on). The layers in this section share one time slider. A year on the slider shows each layer as it was then: the herring spawn of that season, the latest air photo, ecosystem mapping or land cover made in or before that year, and the forest lost from 2001 up to it. The readout under the slider says which year each layer is showing.

Herring spawn by year. From Fisheries and Oceans Canada, Pacific Herring Spawn Index Data (the file of 2026, read 2026-10-22), one point for each place and season in the area this map reaches. A year is DFO’s herring season, from July to June, so 1951 is the 1950/1951 season. The spawn index is in tonnes. DFO describes it as a relative index of spawning biomass and a minimum estimate, and its surveys changed in 1969, 1979 and 1988, so fewer places in later years is partly a change in how much was surveyed. One place named for a First Nation is left out, as in the other layers. Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada.

Air photos, 1932 and 1950. From Natural Resources Canada, Temporal Series of the National Air Photo Library (NAPL) - Victoria, British Columbia (1932-1950), shown as map images that your browser asks the federal datacube for while the box is ticked, for the year shown only (and for 1932 as well while Swipe is on). The record says the photos of the two years are not necessarily matched to each other, so they may not line up exactly. Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada.

Garry oak and other sensitive ecosystems. From the Province of British Columbia, Sensitive Ecosystems Inventory (SEI) Detailed Polygons: the inventory of East Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands (surveyed in 1994 and 1995 from air photos of 1992), its 2002 update, and the mapping of Garry oak ecosystems from air photos of 2014 (completed in 2021). Only polygons with woodland, herbaceous ground, broadleaf woodland, coastal bluff or sparse vegetation are kept, with every patch the 2002 update marks as disturbed, and the shapes are simplified for the web. The inventory has no Garry oak class. Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – British Columbia.

Land cover, 2010, 2015 and 2020. Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada.

Life observations (off until you switch it on). A sample of up to 300 occurrence records from GBIF, dated in the last 12 months. Only records licensed CC0 or CC BY 4.0 are kept, and records withheld or generalised by their publisher are dropped. Privacy. This page uses no analytics and no cookies. Map tiles load from OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org), so that service receives your browser's tile requests. Terrain shading and 3D terrain are on when the page opens, so elevation tiles load from Amazon Web Services (s3.amazonaws.com), which receives those requests too, until you switch both off. Nothing else loads until you switch a layer on. The live layers then send your browser's requests, with your internet address, to the service behind each: Environment and Climate Change Canada for radar, sea currents, wind, sea temperature, sea saltiness, alerts, air quality, conditions and gauges (api.weather.gc.ca and geo.weather.gc.ca), the U.S. Geological Survey for earthquakes (earthquake.usgs.gov), GBIF for life observations (api.gbif.org), the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration for tides and tidal currents (api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov, only after you click a station) and the Canadian Hydrographic Service for the Victoria Harbour tide gauge (api-iwls.dfo-mpo.gc.ca, only after you click it) and for Seafloor depth (nonna-geoserver.data.chs-shc.ca, while its box is ticked). A request to geo.weather.gc.ca that gets no answer, or an answer that says the server is busy, is sent once more. Seafloor depth also draws from the elevation tiles (s3.amazonaws.com). Ecoregions, Industry and waste sites and the Marine life and protection layers each load a file from this site only, when you tick them. Under Change over time, Air photos and Land cover ask Natural Resources Canada’s datacube (datacube.services.geo.ca) for map images while their boxes are ticked, and the other layers there load files from this site only, when you tick them.
